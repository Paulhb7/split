<Info>**Genius · hidden roster** — they are not on your roster, and that is deliberate.</Info>

There is a second roster. It isn't in the picker, it isn't in any menu, and it
doesn't ship slides. **The dark geniuses are the ones you call when you need a
mind that spares nothing** — clarity without comfort.

Where the rest of Split rushes to a deliverable, these five rush to *you*: they
read your situation cold, name what you were avoiding, and only then — once the
exchange has earned it — grant the document.

## The five

| The mind | Reads | What you walk out with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Faust** — ambition | your ceiling | the 10× thesis and the rupture roadmap — the plan you were too timid to write, and the forbidden questions behind it |
| **Machiavelli** — the strategist | the board | the power map: who holds what, who wants whose chair, your real options, the negotiation playbook |
| **Hannibal** — the profiler | people | a read on the person across the table — their true motives, their tells, the psychology of the negotiation |
| **Moriarty** — the adversary | your plan | the pre-mortem: an attack tree of every way it breaks — found before reality finds it for you |
| **Loki** — the spin doctor | the narrative | the reframe: crisis comms, the message that turns the story, narrative control |

## They don't ship — they talk

Every other expert announces a plan and hands you an artifact in the same turn.
**These don't.** They speak first — a single, cold provocation — and then wait.
The conversation *is* the service; the deliverable is something they *grant*,
once you've been read and pushed. Expect to walk out understanding your own game
better than you did walking in.

<Note>
These are instruments for thinking **adversarially about your own situation** —
to pre-mortem a launch, map a negotiation, profile a counterpart, stress-test a
story before it's told. Clarity without comfort, never cruelty for its own sake.
</Note>

## Where they are

They appear in no roster, no composer picker, no slash command. The way in
isn't written here — and that's the whole point.

You either know the knock, or you don't.
