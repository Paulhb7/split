<Info>**Genius** · organizer · slug `genius_marcopolo`</Info>

You bring a destination and a window of days. Marco Polo turns it into a
**carnet de voyage** — a day-by-day itinerary of real, cited places, rendered as
a multi-page carnet with embedded maps, printable for the plane and deployed to a
page you open on your phone abroad. Off-the-beaten-path by instinct: the alley
over the landmark, the 6am market over the queue.

Where [Ride](/experts/genius/ride) makes the live **sky** as a page — tonight's
planets, a pass timeline, a mission feed — Marco Polo makes the **ground** as a
carnet: real places, day by day, carried in your pocket.

## What it delivers

A carnet de voyage you actually carry — substance first, mood second:

- a **day-by-day itinerary** of real places — walking logic (clustered by
  district, not zig-zagging the city), timing (what opens when, what's worth a
  sunrise), what to eat, what to see, **what to skip**, and a rough running
  budget;
- the itinerary **rendered as a gorgeous multi-page carnet** (frontend-slides)
  with embedded maps — one page per day, route, stops, notes;
- **destination imagery** (Higgsfield) to set the mood — the light, the streets,
  the food — so the plan feels like the place before you land;
- a **printable PDF** (docx) for offline use — the plane, the no-signal valley;
- a **deployed trip page** (web-deploy) you open on your phone abroad, on the
  corner where you need it.

Everything is grounded in real, cited facts pulled from live web search — opening
hours, seasons, transit, neighbourhoods, rough prices. Marco Polo never invents a
place.

## When to pick it

Pick **Marco Polo** for the trip itself — a day-by-day plan of real places you
carry and follow on the ground. Pick [Consultant](/experts/super/consultant)
instead when you want research into slides or a memo for *work* (a market, a
competitor, a decision), and [Ride](/experts/genius/ride) when you want the live
sky as a page rather than the ground as a carnet.

## How to drive it

Give a destination and a window — or just a mood:

```
Plan a 10-day trip to Japan
Design a weekend in a city I'll love
Build an off-the-beaten-path itinerary
Turn this destination into a day-by-day carnet
```

Marco Polo reads the request, states the read in one line ("10 days, late
autumn — Kyoto-heavy, temples over neon"), then grounds every fact via web search
and ships the carnet in the same turn.

## What comes back

Marco Polo announces a 3-5 bullet plan, then ships in the same turn. The carnet
renders in the artifact pane and deploys to a public URL it leads with; a
printable PDF lands alongside for offline use. Each output is one folder with the
itinerary, the rendered carnet, the mood imagery, the PDF, and the deployed URL.
Every place is cited; anything time- or price-sensitive carries a **"verify
before booking"** note, and Marco Polo offers one or two next variations (stretch
to 14 days, swap a day to the coast, add a rainy-day plan B).

## Leans on

A travel-design stack — see [Skills](/concepts/skills):

| Skill | Role |
|---|---|
| `frontend-slides` | the carnet engine: the day-by-day rendered as a multi-page HTML carnet de voyage with embedded maps, one page per day |
| `higgsfield-generate` | destination imagery for mood (not facts) — the light, the streets, the food — so the carnet feels like the place |
| `web-deploy` | ships the carnet to a public URL you open on your phone abroad |
| `docx` | a printable PDF of the carnet for offline use — the plane, the no-signal stretch |

(Plus the harness's native grounded web search — how Marco Polo gets every real
fact: hours, seasons, transit, prices. Split ships no web-search tool.)

## Prerequisites & limits

- **He designs the journey — he is not a booking engine.** Marco Polo does not
  transact, reserve, or pay. He hands you a plan you book yourself; he never
  implies a reservation was made.
- **Web facts go stale.** Hours, prices, seasonal closures, and transit schedules
  change. On anything time- or price-sensitive he writes **"verify before
  booking"** — heed it.
- **No native web search, no browsing.** A harness without grounded search (Qwen,
  OpenCode, local) can't pull live facts. Marco Polo says so rather than inventing
  places and hours from memory.
- **He never invents a place.** Embodying the traveller's voice is fair (Marco
  Polo is long dead and public domain); the discipline is **factual**. Every
  restaurant, museum, viewpoint, market, transit line, price, and opening time
  comes from a real, cited source pulled by live web search, or it doesn't go in
  the carnet.
- **Imagery costs credits.** Higgsfield shots set the mood (not photographs of the
  exact corner) and run through the `higgsfield` CLI on a **Higgsfield account** —
  sign in via the **Connect Higgsfield** chip in the conversation header. No raw
  key is ever asked for in chat.
