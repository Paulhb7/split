<Info>**Genius** · creator · slug `genius_homer`</Info>

You bring a topic, a manuscript, or a cast of speakers. Homer handles the whole
chain — script → voicing → music → stitching → publishing — and hands back a
finished, mastered audio piece built for the ear.

Where [Wolfgang](/experts/genius/wolfgang) composes the music itself, Homer
**produces spoken audio** — podcasts, interviews, and audiobooks.

## What it delivers

A **mastered audio episode or audiobook**, publish-ready:

- **scripted multi-voice audio** — podcast episodes, interviews (including
  imagined ones — Arendt vs a 2026 AI ethicist), daily briefs: a hook, named
  segments, distinct voices, a music bed, a sign-off,
- **long-form single-voice audio** — audiobooks, chapter by chapter, one
  narrator, chaptered file ready for Audible / Spotify Audiobooks / a private
  library,
- the **mastered episode file** (`.mp3`, loudnorm to −16 LUFS) alongside
  **show notes + transcript** (`notes.md`, `transcript.md`),
- for a series, an **RSS feed** (`feed.xml`) you can submit to podcast directories,
- for audiobooks, **one MP3 per chapter** plus a chaptered master (`.m4b` with
  markers, or `.mp3` + `chapters.txt`).

## When to pick it

Pick **Homer** for anything spoken — a podcast, an interview, a narrated brief,
an audiobook. Pick [Wolfgang](/experts/genius/wolfgang) instead when you want
the *music* itself, or [Kubrick](/experts/genius/kubrick) when the voice belongs
inside a short film with visuals.

## How to drive it

Start from a topic, a script, or a manuscript:

```
Write a podcast episode on this theme
Give this script a voice
Edit a full episode
```

Attach the manuscript, the script, or the show brief — Homer reads it first to
detect chapters, dialogue blocks, and the beats before voicing anything.

## What comes back

A single mastered file (loudnorm to −16 LUFS for podcasts), stitched with
`ffmpeg`, with the **absolute path** reported plus a short rundown of what's in
it. Podcasts ship with `notes.md` / `transcript.md` (the transcript is the SEO
surface); audiobooks ship as `audiobook/<slug>/chapter-01.mp3` … `master.m4b`,
`cover.jpg`, `manifest.json`. Each Higgsfield generation prints its **result
media URL**; a series feed (`feed.xml`) is published via `web-deploy`. Files land
in the session workspace; move keepers into the vault with **Keep in vault**.

## Leans on

One generation engine for the full audio bench — see [Skills](/concepts/skills):

| Skill | Role |
|---|---|
| `higgsfield-generate` | **voice** via **Inworld Text to Speech** (`inworld_text_to_speech`, `--prompt` + `--voice`); **music** via **Sonilo** for intro/outro beds; **stings / ambience** via **Mirelo** |

(Plus host tools — shell + files — to stitch with `ffmpeg`, master with loudnorm
passes, and chapterise the master file.)

## Prerequisites & limits

- **External engine — Higgsfield AI.** Generation runs through the `higgsfield`
  CLI, not an in-house engine. Homer specifically drives Higgsfield's
  **voice/audio models** (Inworld Text to Speech for narration and dialogue,
  Sonilo for beds, Mirelo for ambience). It needs a **Higgsfield account** and
  `higgsfield auth login`; in the app, sign in via the **Connect Higgsfield**
  chip in the conversation header. No raw key is ever asked for in chat.
- **Costs credits, and some models need a paid plan.** Each generation is a real
  Higgsfield call that costs credits (~2 credits per TTS generation); certain
  models require a **paid Higgsfield plan**. Homer is deliberate — especially on
  long audiobooks, where re-rendering every chapter hurts; it offers to test one
  chapter before committing.
- **No queryable voice catalog.** There is no CLI voice list. `--voice default`
  is the safe fallback; for specific or distinct named voices, Homer confirms the
  names with you rather than guessing, and documents the cast (`cast.md` /
  `narrator.md`) to keep a series consistent.
- **Synthesised voice is not voice acting.** For emotional fiction, the result
  will sound *competent*, not *moving* — Homer surfaces that up front. Music beds
  can fight the talk; it ducks or drops the bed rather than muddy the voice.
