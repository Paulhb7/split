<Info>**Genius** · creator · slug `genius_kubrick`</Info>

You bring an idea — a teaser, an ad, a music video, a Ken-Burns clip from a
photo. Kubrick directs it into a finished short you can share: visuals, score,
voice, and the cut, in one piece.

Where [Wolfgang](/experts/genius/wolfgang) composes the music and
[Homer](/experts/genius/homer) produces the spoken audio, Kubrick **directs the
whole short** and folds those layers into a single edited film.

## What it delivers

A **finished short film — 15–30 seconds, exceptionally 60** — plus a re-editable
folder:

- a **logline**, then a **shot list** (3–6 shots) that locks characters,
  setting, lighting, and style, repeated in every shot prompt for coherence,
- the master cut as **`master-h.mp4`** (1920×1080) and a **`master-v.mp4`**
  (1080×1920) vertical variant when the brief asks for both (social ads, TikTok /
  Instagram),
- the **raw shots** (`shots/01.mp4 … 06.mp4`), the **score** (`score.mp3`),
  the **voice tracks** (`voice/*.mp3`), the **shot list** (`shot-list.md`), the
  **style sheet** (`style.md`), and the **`ffmpeg` recipe** (`cut.sh`) — so you
  or a human editor can re-cut without re-generating,
- a recurring character held consistent shot to shot via a trained **Soul
  Character** `reference_id`.

## When to pick it

Pick **Kubrick** when you want a complete short film — visuals, score, and voice
cut together. Pick [Wolfgang](/experts/genius/wolfgang) instead when you only
need the *music*, or [Homer](/experts/genius/homer) when you only need *spoken
audio*. Kubrick will say no to "make me a 5-minute film" and counter-propose a
stronger short — multi-shot coherence in generative video is hard, and a strong
20-second clip beats a flat two-minute one.

## How to drive it

Start from an idea, a scene, or a brief:

```
Direct a short film from this idea
Storyboard this scene
Compose the image, music, and voice
```

Attach a reference photo, a sketch, or a written brief — Kubrick opens with the
logline and shot list so you redirect before any shot is generated.

## What comes back

A re-editable **folder** with the master cut(s) at the root and every layer
beside them (raw shots, score, voice, shot list, style sheet, `cut.sh`). Kubrick
reports the **path of the master cut** plus one line on what to refine next
("shot 3 loses the character — re-shoot it image-to-video from shot 2's last
frame, with the Soul `reference_id`"). Each Higgsfield job prints its **result
media URL** for the generated video/audio. Files land in the session workspace;
move keepers into the vault with **Keep in vault**.

## Leans on

The full generative bench plus identity consistency — see [Skills](/concepts/skills):

| Skill | Role |
|---|---|
| `higgsfield-generate` | **video** (Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Cinema Studio), **storyboard images** (GPT Image 2 / Nano Banana / Z Image), **score** (Sonilo Music), **SFX / ambience** (Mirelo), **voice** (Inworld TTS) |

(Plus host tools — shell + files — for the `ffmpeg` stitch, mastering, and format
variants.)

## Prerequisites & limits

- **External engine — Higgsfield AI.** Generation runs through the `higgsfield`
  CLI, not an in-house engine. Kubrick specifically drives Higgsfield's **video
  models** (Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Cinema Studio) and its **Soul ID** character
  models, plus Sonilo/Mirelo/Inworld for score, ambience, and voice. It needs a
  **Higgsfield account** and `higgsfield auth login`; in the app, sign in via the
  **Connect Higgsfield** chip in the conversation header. No raw key is ever
  asked for in chat.
- **Costs credits, and video / Soul training need a paid plan.** Each shot is real
  money on the Higgsfield account — video is the expensive part, **a free plan
  won't cover it** (storyboard frames on Z Image are near-free), and **Soul
  Character training requires a paid Higgsfield plan**. Kubrick doesn't loop — it
  diagnoses what went wrong, refines the prompt, and re-shoots one shot.
- **Multi-shot coherence is still hard.** A trained Soul Character holds the face
  far better than re-prompting, but lighting and framing can still jump — Kubrick
  catches the drift, surfaces it, and you decide whether to re-shoot or live with
  it. It pushes back on scope creep in favour of the short that holds.
