# Style recipes — dial in a tradition

Each recipe is a set of dials (key/mode, tempo, metre, left-hand texture,
harmonic palette, melodic gesture) plus the **public-domain master to study**
(see `exemplars.md` for annotated ABC). Match a **tradition or era** — never
imitate a *living* composer (legal + it's lazy). These are starting points;
break them on purpose.

---

## Baroque — invention / counterpoint  *(study: Bach, Vivaldi)*
- **Mode/key**: clear major or minor; modulate to the dominant or relative.
- **Tempo/metre**: steady, motoric. 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 12/8. ♩=60–120, *no rubato*.
- **Texture**: **two independent lines** — RH and LH are equals (not melody +
  accompaniment). The LH **walks** and imitates the RH (the subject answered an
  octave lower a bar later = imitation).
- **Harmony**: strictly functional; sequences everywhere; circle-of-fifths
  drives; strong cadences with suspensions.
- **Gesture**: one **subject** stated, then sequenced, inverted, fragmented.
  Continuous spinning-out (*Fortspinnung*) — rarely stops to breathe.
- *Exemplar*: a Bach two-part invention / chorale phrase; Vivaldi *Spring* opening
  (ritornello + sequence).

## Classical — period style  *(study: Mozart, early Beethoven)*
- **Mode/key**: major-leaning, bright; clear tonic.
- **Tempo/metre**: 4/4, 3/4, 2/4. ♩=80–140.
- **Texture**: **melody + accompaniment**. LH = **Alberti bass** (broken
  triad: low-high-mid-high) or simple chords.
- **Harmony**: I–IV–V–I clarity; balanced, frequent cadences; harmonic rhythm
  slow then quickening into cadences.
- **Gesture**: **periods** (antecedent → half cadence, consequent → authentic
  cadence); tidy 4- and 8-bar phrases; motivic development.
- *Exemplar*: Beethoven *Ode to Joy* (stepwise arch), *Für Elise* (neighbor-tone
  figure + period).

## Romantic — nocturne  *(study: Chopin, John Field)*
- **Mode/key**: minor or warm major; lots of chromatic color.
- **Tempo/metre**: slow, singing. 3/4, 4/4, 6/8. ♩=50–76, **rubato** (mark it).
- **Texture**: a **singing RH melody** over a **wide broken-chord LH** (arpeggios
  spanning a 10th, sustained with pedal). One hand sings, one shimmers.
- **Harmony**: rich — 7ths/9ths, secondary dominants, borrowed minor iv,
  Neapolitan, the occasional deceptive cadence to ache.
- **Gesture**: long-breathed melody with **appoggiaturas and suspensions** at the
  peaks; ornaments (turns, grace notes); the climax late and high.
- *Exemplar*: a Chopin nocturne theme (see `exemplars.md`).

## Impressionist — color over function  *(study: Debussy, Ravel)*
- **Mode/scale**: **modal** (Dorian, Lydian, Mixolydian), **pentatonic**,
  **whole-tone**. Not major/minor functional.
- **Tempo/metre**: fluid, often slow; flexible metre.
- **Texture**: **parallel chords (planing)** — move a whole chord shape in
  parallel; pedal washes; widely spaced sonorities; ostinato pedal tones.
- **Harmony**: **non-functional** — chords chosen for *color*, not progression;
  unresolved 7ths/9ths/11ths; no driving V→I.
- **Gesture**: floating, static-but-shimmering; melody often pentatonic fragments.
- **⚠ Honesty**: this style **fits ABC poorly** (dense, non-functional) and is a
  weak model for *melodic clarity*. Study it for **harmonic color**, not for
  learning to write a singable line. For a real Debussy/Ravel texture you'll be
  approximating heavily.

## Satie-flavoured — gentle melancholy  *(study: Satie Gymnopédies)*
- **Mode/key**: modal major with a wistful tint; slow.
- **Tempo/metre**: 3/4, very slow (♩=60). Spacious.
- **Texture**: simple — a bare LH (bass note → soft chord, "oom-pah" but tender)
  under a plain, slow RH melody.
- **Harmony**: lush **maj7 / add9** chords, gentle unprepared dissonance, plagal
  color; little functional drive.
- **Gesture**: long notes, lots of rest/space, an unhurried stepwise melody.

## Virtuoso — caprice / variations  *(study: Paganini)*
- **Mode/key**: minor for fire (A minor), major for brilliance.
- **Tempo/metre**: fast, brilliant; 2/4, 6/8.
- **Texture**: dazzling RH **figuration** — arpeggios, scales, leaps, repeated
  notes — over a lean LH.
- **Harmony**: clear functional frame so the fireworks have a grid.
- **Gesture**: a crisp **theme**, then **variations** that re-clothe it (faster
  notes, register changes, mode flip).
- *Exemplar*: Paganini *Caprice 24* theme (clean 16-bar period, then variations).

## Lo-fi / contemporary calm
- **Mode/key**: minor or modal; mellow.
- **Tempo/metre**: 4/4, slow, slight swing. ♩=70–85.
- **Texture**: simple looped chord progression, sparse melody, space.
- **Harmony**: **maj7, min7, min9** chords; ii–V loops; no urgency.
- **Gesture**: short repeating motif, lots of rest, a "head-nod" groove.

## Lullaby / folk
- **Mode/key**: simple major or pentatonic.
- **Tempo/metre**: 6/8 or 3/4, gentle.
- **Texture**: rocking LH (low-chord-chord), plain RH.
- **Harmony**: I–IV–V only; rock between I and V.
- **Gesture**: small range, very stepwise, repetitive, soothing.

---

### How to pick
Brief says *"calm / sad / intimate"* → Satie, nocturne, lo-fi.
*"Grand / heroic / structured"* → Classical period, Baroque.
*"Brilliant / showy"* → virtuoso/caprice.
*"Dreamy / floating / colorful"* → Impressionist (accept the ABC limits).
When in doubt: a **Classical period in a clear key** is the safest beautiful
default — and the easiest to engrave cleanly.
