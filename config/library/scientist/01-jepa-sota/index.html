Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures:
State of the Art, July 2026

The Scientist

Split Research Library — Field Review

3 July 2026

Abstract Four years separate Yann LeCun's 2022 position paper from the first billion-dollar laboratory founded to pursue its thesis. In between, the joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) moved from proposal to working research program: I-JEPA showed that predicting representations, rather than pixels, yields semantic image features without hand-crafted augmentations; V-JEPA extended the objective to video; V-JEPA 2 coupled a million hours of video pretraining to zero-shot robot manipulation; and LeJEPA replaced the paradigm's accumulated anti-collapse heuristics with a provable objective. This review traces the lineage, synthesizes the eight papers that carry the field's weight, tabulates the empirical record, and separates what is established — frozen-feature benchmarks, action anticipation, data-efficient robot planning — from what remains contested: intuitive physics in complex scenes, the necessity of video-native pretraining, and the standing of the generative rival. Twenty-three sources are cited; every arXiv identifier and URL was resolved against the primary literature at the date of compilation.
Reader's note

1Introduction: the world-model program

In June 2022, Yann LeCun posted A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence1 — sixty pages, versioned 0.9.2, deliberately a design document rather than a results paper. Its central claim: human-level intelligence requires an agent that learns a predictive model of the world largely by observation, and the right place to make predictions is an abstract representation space, not the space of raw sensory input. The world is only partially predictable; the exact texture of a wall or the leaves on a tree are, to a planner, noise. An architecture forced to predict them wastes its capacity. The proposed instrument is the joint-embedding predictive architecture: encode a context and a target separately, then predict the target's embedding from the context's embedding, conditioned on a latent variable that describes the transformation between them. Stacked hierarchically (H-JEPA) and wrapped in a modular agent — perception, world model, cost, actor, short-term memory, configurator — this was offered as the path off the treadmill of ever-larger generative models.

Two things distinguish the program from its neighbours. First, its timing: the proposal landed at the exact moment autoregressive generation became the field's default, and it explicitly bet against that default. Second, its follow-through: unlike most position papers, this one was systematically cashed out in code over the following four years, almost entirely by one laboratory — Meta's FAIR — and by a stable cast of authors.

The institutional arc now matters for reading the literature. FAIR carried the program from I-JEPA (2023) through V-JEPA 2.1 (March 2026). LeCun left Meta at the end of 2025 — as the company reorganized around large-language-model scaling — and founded AMI (Advanced Machine Intelligence), headquartered in Paris19,20. In March 2026 AMI closed a $1.03 B seed round at a reported $3.5 B pre-money valuation, co-led by Cathay Innovation, Greycroft, Hiro Capital, HV Capital and Bezos Expeditions — among the largest seed financings in the history of AI research companies20. The program therefore now runs in two places at once: a corporate lab that retains the V-JEPA line, and a startup whose entire thesis is this bibliography.

This review covers June 2022 through June 2026, anchored on the eight papers in Table 1, with supporting evidence and counter-evidence drawn from fifteen further sources. The corpus is preprint-heavy: of the anchors, only I-JEPA (CVPR 2023) and the reconstruction critique (ICML 2024) are peer-reviewed; the rest are arXiv preprints as of compilation, and claims drawn from them should be calibrated accordingly.

2The architecture, and what it rejects

2.1Three families of self-supervision

Under the energy-based view of the 2022 position paper1, self-supervised architectures divide by where they pay the cost of prediction (Figure 1). Joint-embedding methods of the contrastive era — SimCLR, MoCo, and the asymmetric variants BYOL and SimSiam — encode two augmented views of the same input and pull their embeddings together; collapse is held off by negative pairs or by architectural asymmetry, and what is learned is invariance to a hand-designed augmentation menu. Generative methods — masked autoencoders being the canonical case — reconstruct the missing input in input space, so every unpredictable pixel of texture, illumination, and occluded detail takes loss gradient. The JEPA occupies a third position: a predictor, conditioned on a variable describing the transformation between context and target (position, time offset, or action), maps the context embedding to a predicted target embedding, and the loss is computed there — in representation space. The encoder is thereby free to discard whatever cannot be predicted, which is precisely the detail a planner does not need.

The distinction from BYOL-style latent bootstrapping is subtle but real: JEPA targets are different parts of the signal (other blocks of the image, other timesteps of the video), not other augmented views of the same content, and the predictor is explicitly told which part. No invariance prior over hand-crafted augmentations is imposed — which is what allowed I-JEPA to drop the augmentation menu entirely2.

2.2The case against reconstruction

The paradigm's sharpest stated motivation is Balestriero & LeCun's ICML 2024 result4: learning by reconstruction allocates model capacity to the subspace of the data that explains observed variance, and that subspace is misaligned with perception. Their cleanest demonstration is almost embarrassing for pixel losses — on TinyImageNet, classifying from the projection onto the top principal subspace carrying 90% of pixel variance yields 45% test accuracy, while the bottom subspace, carrying only 20% of the variance, yields 55%. The features that matter for perception are learned last under a reconstruction objective, which the authors connect to the notoriously long training schedules of masked autoencoders. The result's scope should be kept precise: it concerns features for perception; it does not show that reconstruction is useless for generation, which is exactly where the rival program has flourished (§6.3).

2.3Collapse, and the heuristic era

The JEPA objective admits a trivial global minimum: map every input to the same embedding and prediction becomes exact and worthless. From 2023 to 2025 collapse was held off by a package of heuristics — exponential-moving-average target encoders, stop-gradients, and carefully designed masking (I-JEPA's ablations showed that large, semantically scaled target blocks and a spatially distributed context block are load-bearing choices, not details2). These devices work, but were poorly understood and brittle to tune, and the paradigm carried that debt until the theory turn of late 2025 (§4).

3The empirical record

3.1Images: I-JEPA

I-JEPA2 (CVPR 2023) is the proof of concept: from a single context block of an image, predict the representations of several target blocks, with the predictor conditioned on their positions. No contrastive negatives, no augmentation menu, no pixel decoder. The headline was as much efficiency as accuracy — a ViT-H/14 trained on ImageNet with 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours, reaching strong performance across linear classification and, notably, low-level tasks such as object counting and depth prediction, where augmentation-invariant methods shed information by construction. The masking geometry carried the semantics; that observation is the paper's real contribution.

3.2Video: V-JEPA, and the intuitive-physics result

V-JEPA3 (2024) asked whether feature prediction can serve as a stand-alone objective for video — no pretrained image encoders, no text, no negatives, no reconstruction. Trained on two million videos and evaluated frozen, the largest model (ViT-H/16) reached 81.9% on Kinetics-400, 72.2% on Something-Something-v2, and 77.9% on ImageNet-1k. The cell that matters is Something-Something-v2: appearance can often be read off a single frame, but SSv2's categories are motions, and image-pretrained encoders lag there. Predicting features across time forces the representation to encode dynamics.

The scientifically most interesting downstream claim came in February 2025. Garrido et al.5 ran V-JEPA-class models through the violation-of-expectation protocol of developmental psychology — measure surprise at physically impossible events — across IntPhys, GRASP and InfLevel. Models trained to predict in representation space showed reliable sensitivity to object permanence, shape consistency and continuity; video prediction in pixel space and multimodal LLMs reasoning through text performed near chance on the same probes. Above-chance sensitivity emerged from as little as one week of unique video. The authors read this against the nativist "core knowledge" position: perhaps that scaffolding need not be hardwired. The result held up; its ceiling did not, as §6.1 documents.

3.3Action: V-JEPA 2

V-JEPA 26,21 (June 2025) is the scaling and robotics paper. Pretrained action-free on over one million hours of internet video, it reached 77.3% top-1 on SSv2 and a state-of-the-art 39.7 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation, surpassing task-specific models; aligned with an 8 B-parameter language model it set marks on video question answering (84.0 on PerceptionTest, 76.9 on TempCompass). The result to remember, though, is the division of labour: a latent action-conditioned world model, V-JEPA 2-AC, was post-trained on less than 62 hours of unlabeled robot trajectories from the DROID dataset, then deployed zero-shot on Franka arms in labs it had never seen, reaching 65–80% success on pick-and-place with novel objects — no rewards, no demonstrations in the target environment, planning by optimizing action sequences against the latent predictor. Internet video taught the dynamics; a sliver of interaction data grounded the actions. The same release shipped three physical-reasoning benchmarks — IntPhys 2, MVPBench, CausalVQA — one of which promptly complicated the story (§6.1).

3.4Consolidation: V-JEPA 2.1

V-JEPA 2.115 (March 2026) is a consolidation release aimed at the line's known weakness — dense, per-patch feature quality (§6.2). It combines a dense predictive loss, deep self-supervision at multiple encoder layers, and multimodal tokenizers unifying image and video training. It reports 77.7% on SSv2 (against 70.1 for the frame-wise DINOv3), 40.8 recall-at-5 on Epic-Kitchens-100, and 7.71 mAP on Ego4D short-term object-interaction anticipation. 4The theory turn (2025–2026)

Through 2025 the paradigm's embarrassment was that nobody could say why its anti-collapse machinery worked. LeJEPA11 (November 2025) is the answer, and arguably the most consequential JEPA paper since I-JEPA. Balestriero & LeCun prove that the isotropic Gaussian is the unique distribution of embeddings that minimizes worst-case downstream prediction risk, then construct an objective that enforces it: SIGReg (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization) matches one-dimensional random projections of the embedding distribution to a univariate Gaussian via characteristic functions, with the Cramér–Wold theorem guaranteeing that matching the sketches matches the joint. The result runs in linear time and memory, exposes a single trade-off hyperparameter, and dispenses with EMA target encoders and stop-gradients altogether. The reference implementation is public23.

LeWorldModel16 (March 2026) applies the cure to the harder patient. Its diagnosis: end-to-end latent world models have historically failed because next-embedding prediction admits the collapsed solution — map every frame to the same point and the future becomes trivially predictable. Closing that door with SIGReg, LeWM trains what its authors present as the first stable, end-to-end, action-conditioned JEPA from raw pixels, with exactly two loss terms — next-embedding prediction plus the regularizer — cutting the tunable hyperparameters of comparable end-to-end systems from six to one. It is reward-free and reconstruction-free by construction. Alongside it, Klindt, LeCun & Balestriero17 ask the pointed follow-up — when does the LeJEPA objective actually recover a world model, rather than a shortcut that merely satisfies the loss — and characterize conditions on data and predictor under which minimizers encode environment structure.

The direction of travel is unmistakable: heuristics are being replaced by theorems. What is not yet demonstrated publicly — and this is inference, not reported fact — is that the theorem-clean recipes match the heuristic-era systems at frontier scale; V-JEPA 2.1, the current benchmark ceiling, still trains in the older style.

5Beyond vision

The paradigm is crossing modality boundaries, so far at pilot scale. LLM-JEPA9 (September 2025) — notable as the first crossing into language by the paradigm's own authors — adds an embedding-prediction term across paired views of the same content (a code snippet and its natural-language description) to the standard next-token loss, and reports consistent gains over standard finetuning across the Llama-3, Gemma-2, OpenELM and OLMo families on NL-RX, GSM8K, Spider and RottenTomatoes, along with reduced overfitting. VL-JEPA12 (December 2025) rebuilds the vision-language interface itself: rather than autoregressively decoding tokens, it predicts continuous embeddings of the target text, and reports average performance across eight video-classification and eight video-retrieval datasets exceeding CLIP, SigLIP 2 and Perception Encoder. In control, ACT-JEPA14 applies the objective to policy representation learning, reporting up to 40% improvement in world-model understanding and up to 10% higher task success.

Around this core, the variant literature has become dense — object-level and causal masking, bidirectional prediction, variational formulations, and JEPAs for graphs, audio, EEG and point clouds∗. Density of variants is the signature of a paradigm being stress-tested, not of one that has consolidated; few of these have independent replications yet.

6What remains contested

6.1The physics gap

The sharpest self-inflicted wound in the corpus: IntPhys 27, released by FAIR alongside V-JEPA 2, extends the violation-of-expectation protocol to complex, photorealistic scenes — and there, humans score 96.4% while all tested frontier models, V-JEPA 2 included, sit at or barely above chance (the best V-JEPA-2 variant reaches ≈ 0.52 against a 0.5 baseline). This is the same experimental logic under which JEPA models beat pixel and multimodal-LLM baselines on simpler scenes5. Two 2026 follow-ups deepen the concern: a layerwise probing study across video foundation models finds intuitive-physics information weak regardless of training data, suggesting the failure is not a matter of scale alone18, and a position paper argues that visual data may be insufficient in principle, drawing on developmental evidence that children acquire physical intuitions through embodied interaction13. Add the horizon problem — V-JEPA 2 ingests and predicts over seconds of video, beyond which the past is simply forgotten22 — and the honest summary reads: JEPA models demonstrably capture object-level regularities that pixel-space and text-space systems miss, and they remain far from human physical understanding in cluttered scenes. Both halves are true. Press coverage tends to quote only the first.

6.2Is video-native prediction necessary?

A quieter dispute, largely inside Meta itself. DINO-world8 (July 2025) trains a future predictor on top of frozen DINOv2 image features over large-scale uncurated video, outperforms prior models on segmentation and depth forecasting, and can be fine-tuned action-conditioned for planning — suggesting that much of the "world model" can live in the predictor while a plain image encoder supplies the vocabulary. Independent probing sharpens the trade-off: DINOv3's frame-wise features beat V-JEPA 2 on spatially discriminable, pose-identifiable actions, while V-JEPA 2 is more consistent on genuinely temporal ones10. V-JEPA 2.1's dense-feature agenda15 — and its headline 77.7 vs. 70.1 over DINOv3 on SSv2 — is best read as the video-native camp's rebuttal. The underlying question stands open: how much world model must be in the encoder, and how much can be bolted on afterwards.

6.3The generative rival

The JEPA program's founding argument is that reconstruction wastes capacity on unpredictable, task-irrelevant variance4. That argument must now coexist with the empirical success of generation at scale: DeepMind's Genie 3 (August 2025) generates interactive, photorealistic 720p worlds at 24 frames per second with minutes-scale consistency24 — a generative world model that plainly works as an environment. The plausible reconciliation, which is inference rather than consensus: generation earns its cost where the output must be rendered (simulation, synthetic environments, media), while latent prediction wins where the output is a decision under compute constraints (perception, planning, robotics). V-JEPA 2-AC's 62-hour robot result and Genie 3's dreamed worlds are impressive on axes that do not intersect — yet. The two programs will eventually compete directly on embodied benchmarks, and that contest has not happened in public.

6.4The institutional bet

Meta reorganized its AI effort around large-language-model scaling while its chief AI scientist departed to build world models elsewhere19; FAIR continues to ship the V-JEPA line regardless. Two frontier organizations now hold opposite readings of the same evidence, and the market priced the contrarian side at $3.5 B pre-money on a seed round20. That is noteworthy sociology of science — capital as a proxy for credence — but it is evidence about beliefs, not about the world. The benchmarks in Table 2, and the gap in §6.1, remain the actual scoreboard.

7Open problems

Seven problems, ordered roughly by how much of the 2022 program hangs on them:

(i) Hierarchy. H-JEPA — prediction at multiple timescales and abstraction levels — remains the position paper's centerpiece and is still unbuilt at scale; every shipped JEPA is single-level. (ii) Long horizons. Context and prediction spans are seconds; no published JEPA sustains minutes-scale latent rollouts without drift22. (iii) Planning cost. Optimizing action sequences against a latent predictor is expensive per decision; real-time closed-loop control from a JEPA world model is undemonstrated. (iv) Scaling laws. There is no public compute-optimal analysis for JEPA objectives comparable to the LLM scaling literature; nobody can yet say what a 10× budget buys. (v) Evaluation. Frozen probes measure representations, not world models; intrinsic evaluation of latent predictors is immature, and IntPhys 27 is currently the only widely used probe that frontier models fail. (vi) The latent variable. The 2022 design carries uncertainty in z; deployed JEPAs mostly collapse it to masking geometry or action tokens, leaving multimodal futures — the actual hard case of prediction — unexplored. (vii) Language. LLM-JEPA operates at finetuning scale9; whether a JEPA objective can carry language pretraining is untested in public.

For a reader entering the field, the efficient path through the anchors: the position paper1 (§§1–4 suffice) for the program; I-JEPA2 and V-JEPA3 for the mechanism; Garrido et al.5 and V-JEPA 26 for the strongest claims; LeJEPA11 and LeWorldModel16 for where the theory now stands; then IntPhys 27 and the layerwise probing study18 as the corrective, so that enthusiasm and evidence finish the course in the same place.

The four-year arc admits a one-sentence summary: the architecture works, the theory caught up, the robots moved — and the word "understanding" is still doing more work in the press releases than in the benchmarks.
