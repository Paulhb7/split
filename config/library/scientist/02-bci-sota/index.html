Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 02 — Brief

What this atlas is, and how I built it

The exec-summary header that travels with the file — so a reader months from now has the frame without the conversation.

Why

Implants read the brain beautifully but cost surgery, so their reach is ~100 humans. Non-invasive BCI is the wedge that scales to millions — and in 2025-26 it stopped being a toy. This maps where it actually stands.

Context

Field = non-invasive neural read-out for BCI. Period = 2023 → July 2026, weighted to the last 12 months. Included: EEG, fNIRS, OPM-MEG, functional ultrasound (flagged as less-invasive), plus decoders and foundation models.

How to use it

Read left to right as plates: modalities → the decoding leaps → who's building → a performance table → the hard limits → a 5-year line. Every dated claim is cited on Plate 13; arrow keys navigate.

What I assumed

Audience = a technical peer deciding where to place attention or capital. I favoured measured, cited numbers over hype, flagged preprints as preprints, and dropped any claim I could not verify against a July-2026 source.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 05 — Decoding I
Brain-to-text · without surgery

Meta's Brain2Qwerty is the headline result

A convolutional encoder reads MEG while a person types; a transformer models the temporal structure; a character-level language model constrains the output toward plausible text. No implant.

  • v1 (Feb 2025) — MEG character error rate 32% on average, 19% for the best participant; EEG far worse at 67%. 35 volunteers.
  • v2 (Jun 2026) — 61% word accuracy (39% WER), best participant 78%, over half of sentences within one word error. 9 participants, ~22k sentences.
  • The jump: prior non-invasive methods sat near 8% word accuracy. This is the biggest single leap in the modality.
▲ Reads typed / attempted text under MEG in a shielded room — not free inner speech in the wild. v2 data under embargo until publication.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 06 — Decoding II
Speech, semantics & imagery

Decoding meaning is further along than decoding words

Semantic reconstruction
UT Austin · Huth lab · fMRI
A decoder recovers the gist of perceived speech, imagined speech and even silent video — a single model across tasks. Needs ~16 h/subject training and active cooperation. Nature Neuroscience, 2023.
Imagined-speech EEG
Multiple labs · scalp EEG
On constrained vocabularies, deep models (CNN+TCN, GRUs) report high in-lab classification accuracy. But open-vocabulary imagined speech from EEG remains unsolved — the review consensus for 2026.
Overt-speech robustness
Confidence-aware decoding · EEG
2025-26 work adds confidence estimates so a decoder can abstain rather than emit garbage — a practical step toward BCIs that fail safely instead of confidently wrong.

The pattern. Non-invasive decoders are good at the low-dimensional signal — "which of N options", or the semantic drift of a passage — and weak at the high-dimensional one: reconstructing free, novel inner language word-for-word. Cooperation is required to decode, which is also the field's main privacy guardrail.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 07 — Foundation models
The "neural language" bet

Pretrain on oceans of EEG, then decode with a small head

The transformer recipe reached neural data: mask-and-predict pretraining on unlabelled recordings, then fine-tune for a paradigm. A 2026 survey (Liu et al.) catalogs 50 models and benchmarks 12 across 13 datasets and 9 BCI paradigms.

  • Lineage: BENDR → Brant → LaBraM → NeuroLM → EEGPT → CBraMod — the EEG foundation-model canon.
  • Sobering finding: bigger is not yet better — larger models don't reliably generalize more, and specialists trained from scratch stay competitive. Data, not parameters, is the bottleneck.
  • Live application: an EEG FM improved online directional motor-imagery control in 2026 — evidence the pretraining actually transfers to closed-loop use.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 10 — Applications
Where the read-out earns its keep

Three tiers, from clinic to consumer

Tier 1 · Clinical
Diagnose & restore
  • OPM-MEG for epilepsy source-mapping and pre-surgical work-up (Phase-2 trials, 2024-25).
  • Communication for locked-in and ALS patients via spellers.
  • Neurofeedback for stroke rehab and attention disorders.
Tier 2 · Assistive & AR
Control & augment
  • Hands-free control of cursors, wheelchairs, AR interfaces (Cognixion).
  • Real-time game control demonstrated non-invasively (LumiMind, CES 2026).
  • Silent-command layers for wearables via imagined selection.
Tier 3 · Consumer & research
Sense & optimize
  • Sleep guidance — LumiSleep steers toward the sleep-onset pattern in real time.
  • Cognitive-health tracking (attention, fatigue) — Neurable's licensing play.
  • Pharma & neuroscience read-outs via TD-fNIRS (Kernel).

The near-term money is in Tier 3 (monitoring), the near-term impact is in Tier 1 (clinical). Tier 2 control is where the two meet — and where the accuracy bar bites hardest.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 11 — Hard limits
What the physics won't give back

The four walls

  • Signal-to-noise. Scalp signals are micro-volts buried under muscle, eye-blink and line noise. The skull smears and attenuates before you ever record.
  • Calibration & drift. EEG is non-stationary — attention, fatigue, electrode shift and day-to-day variance mean a decoder trained at 9am degrades by noon. Cross-subject transfer is worse.
  • Spatial blur. Volume conduction spreads each source across many electrodes; you lose the fine cortical geometry an implant sees directly.
  • The scaling wall. For neural foundation models, more parameters aren't buying more generalization yet — the corpus, not the network, is the limit.
▲ Cooperation is required to decode — a real capability limit, and the field's built-in privacy guardrail.

Non-invasive BCI · Field Atlas Plate 12 — Trajectory
Where the line points · 2026 → 2031

The five-year read

'26
Now. MEG brain-to-text at 61%; EEG FMs enter online control; consumer sleep & state products ship.
'27
Calibration shrinks. Cross-subject FMs cut per-user setup from hours toward minutes.
'28
OPM scales. 384-channel wearable MEG, cheaper shielding; frequency-tagging BCIs mature.
'29
Hybrids win. EEG+fNIRS+eye fusion pushes robust intent detection into the field.
'30
Encroachment. Non-invasive begins entering tasks that needed implants — for the easier decode targets.
'31
Governance. Neural-data privacy & "mental integrity" law becomes a gating factor, not an afterthought.
The bet that pays: generalist neural foundation models that decode a new user in minutes, not hours.

The honest caveat: verbatim inner-speech read-out and calibration-free control remain unsolved, and the SNR wall is physics, not engineering. Expect steady encroachment on the easy decodes, not a step-change to mind-reading. Watch the calibration curve — it's the field's true progress bar.
