About this atlas

Why

A single-page portrait of the confirmed-exoplanet catalogue: what thirty-four years of planet-hunting looks like when you chart all of it at once.

Context

Data is a snapshot of the NASA Exoplanet Archive's Planetary Systems Composite table (one best-value row per planet), fetched July 2026 and embedded in this file. No live queries; the page is fully self-contained.

How to use

Scroll top to bottom. Hover any bar, point, row or card for detail. Press play on the timeline; filter the radius–period map by detection method.

What we assumed

"Temperate rocky" is a deliberately rough cut — equilibrium temperature 180–310 K and radius 0.5–1.8 R⊕ — and ignores atmospheres. Catalogue composite values are shown without error bars. A handful of ultra-wide imaged companions sit off-chart.

How we find them

Almost no exoplanet has ever been seen. We find worlds by their side-effects — a star that dims, wobbles, brightens, or misses a beat. Each technique has its own blind spots, and its own reach.

All 11 detection methods on record · bar length on a square-root scale so the rare ones stay visible · hover for how each works.

How far each method reaches. Median distance of its discoveries: the Doppler wobble and direct imaging work close to home; transit surveys sweep the middle distance; microlensing sees worlds most of the way to the galactic core — once, and never again.
