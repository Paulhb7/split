Diego's read

Why it worked, where it broke, and the one thing I'd have changed at half-time.

The verdict

Listen. Morocco played the braver football — seventy per cent of the ball is not an accident, it is a plan carried out by men who trust each other. They pinned the Dutch back and made Hakimi the whole right wing. That is beautiful, and it is correct.

But football punishes the team that dominates and does not kill. Two shots on target for the Netherlands — two — and one of them, Gakpo's, was enough to lead in the 72nd minute. Morocco needed Issa Diop, a centre-half, to head them level in stoppage time. When you are that much better and you still need your defender's forehead, the game is telling you something.

And then the lottery. Both sides shook — the bar, the post, Bounou's hands. That is not tactics any more, that is nerve. Morocco had more of it. Deserved on the ball; earned on the penalty spot.

— Diego

◆ Why the plan worked

The moment Koeman went to a back three with Van de Ven at left wing-back, he handed Morocco a target. Hakimi and Brahim Díaz doubled up that side, dragged the wing-back inside-out, and turned 70% possession into a slow suffocation. The Dutch couldn't press three-versus-two in build-up, so they sat — and sitting against Morocco means corners, second balls, and Diop arriving late. That's exactly the goal they conceded.

◆ Where it breaks

Possession without a knife is a trap. Morocco funnelled everything wide and crossed into a box owned by Van Dijk and Aké — 6ft giants who win the first header all day. Five on target from all that territory is thin. And a team that presses high leaves Gakpo one pass from Summerville behind the line — which is the exact move that scored. Control the game, gift the counter: that's the tightrope.

✎ The one adjustment I'd make

Stop crossing into the tall men. At half-time I move Brahim Díaz permanently inside off the right, let Hakimi own the touchline alone, and I tell Saibari to attack the near-post cut-back, not the far-post header. Van Dijk can't head a ball that never goes airborne. Pull it back along the floor to the penalty spot and you score in the 60th, not the shootout.
