About this piece — design case study · interactive prototype · fake data ▾

Why

A portfolio case study for Marée, a concept iOS app for sea swimmers — to show the product direction, not to ship code.

Context

Tides, water temperature and safe-swim windows for coastal people. Four screens, one calm marine design system.

How to use it

The screens are live — the bottom tab bar switches views, and tapping a day on the Tides chart redraws the curve. Explore them.

What I assumed

French-Atlantic coast, metric units, a solo morning swimmer. Data is illustrative. Page dressing is "studio greige"; the app is its own marine world.

The direction: read the sea, not a dashboard.

A swimmer's real question is small — can I go, and when? Everything else is context for that one call. So the app answers it first, on the Now screen, in a single line: a swim window and a verdict. The tide curve, temperature and wind sit under it as evidence, not as the headline.

The system is marine but restrained. A warm sand paper ground keeps it calm and un-clinical; deep-water ink and one tidal teal carry every action and every data line. Numbers are the loudest thing on screen — tide heights, temperatures, coefficients — because that is what a swimmer trusts. The chrome gets out of the way.

Margin note — the "swim window" is the whole product. If a swimmer only ever reads that one line and trusts it, Marée has done its job. Every other screen earns the trust behind it.

On the palette — teal, not blue. Consumer weather apps are a sea of the same sky-blue; a slightly green tidal teal reads like shallow coastal water and, more usefully, owns a shelf nobody else stands on.

On temperature — shown as “feels” beside the raw reading. 18° in a light wind is a different swim than 18° flat, and swimmers know it — so the app admits it too.
Decisions
01 — Type

One grotesk, carried by weight

Hanken Grotesk does the whole app: 700 for the numbers that matter, 500 for labels, a single italic for the human voice in the journal. A humanist grotesk stays warm at small sizes — right for a companion, wrong-feeling only if it tried to be a cockpit.

02 — Colour

Sand ground, tidal teal, two signals

Surfaces are warm off-white, not cold grey. Teal is the only interactive colour. Two status colours and nothing more: a calm green for “good to swim”, a coral-amber for caution. Colour means something every time it appears.

03 — Motion & shape

Soft cards, quiet transitions

Generous 18px radii and low, wide shadows make each card feel like a smooth stone. Screen changes cross-fade in a third of a second — present but never showy. The sea is the drama; the interface is calm.
