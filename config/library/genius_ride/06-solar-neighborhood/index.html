The solar neighborhood

111 stars within 20 light-years, placed at their true positions and coloured by real spectral type. Drag to look, scroll to zoom, click a star.
About this What you're flying through, and where the numbers come from.

Why. Distances to the nearest stars are abstract until you can move around them. This piece takes a real catalogue and puts every neighbour where it actually is in space, so the shape of the Sun's corner of the galaxy — mostly faint red dwarfs, a few bright landmarks — becomes something you can see, not just read. Built by Ride for the Split library.

Context. Positions, distances, spectral types and brightnesses are the real values from the HYG database v41 (built on Hipparcos, Gliese/GJ and Yale catalogues), epoch J2000. Each star's colour is derived from its measured spectral class — O/B blue-white through to M red — so the palette is the data. Sizes track luminosity. The Sun sits at the origin.

How to use it. Orbit with a drag, zoom with the wheel or a pinch. Hover for a name, click for a dossier — distance, type, and any known planets. The list at lower-left is the nearest ten; the rings are distance shells at 5, 10, 15 and 20 light-years. Nothing here re-fetches: the catalogue is baked in, so it renders offline.

What I assumed. A 20-light-year cut (111 neighbours + the Sun). Multiple stars in tight pairs (Alpha Centauri, Sirius A/B) can overlap on screen — that's real, they're that close. Planet notes are limited to systems with confirmed or notable candidate worlds, verified against Wikipedia's nearest-stars tables on 3 July 2026. Faint components keep their catalogue designations.
