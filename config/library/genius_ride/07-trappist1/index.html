TRAPPIST‑1 · Seven Worlds

A red‑dwarf orrery, to scale
An ultracool dwarf star 40.7 light‑years away, in Aquarius — seven roughly Earth‑sized planets in tight, resonant orbits. All seven would fit inside Mercury's orbit of our Sun.
About this view ▼

Why

To see the TRAPPIST‑1 system as it actually is — a cool red star lit in its true colour, and seven small worlds moving at their real orbital periods, rendered to real relative scale.

Context

Every number here is the measured value from the NASA Exoplanet Archive (default parameter set) and Gaia — planet radii, periods, orbital distances, equilibrium temperatures, and the star's radius, temperature and mass. Nothing is invented. The equilibrium temperatures assume no atmosphere; real surfaces could differ.

How to use it

Drag to orbit the camera, scroll to zoom. Click a planet for its dossier. Use the dock to pause / speed up the orbits and to toggle to‑scale spacing (real orbits are so tight the outer planets crowd the star — the legible mode spreads them so you can read them).

What I assumed

Planet sizes are true relative to each other and to the star. Orbit spacing defaults to legible (labelled in the dock); flip to real to feel how cramped the true system is. Time is compressed so a full inner orbit takes a couple of seconds. Colours are naturalistic by equilibrium temperature — hot rock, temperate, and icy — not photographs. — Ride
