How to use this draft — five lines

  1. Verify the party details in the preamble against each company’s Kbis extract — every identifier here is illustrative.
  2. Decide the bracketed option at Article 9 (non-solicitation): keep it or strike it, and have both signatories initial the choice.
  3. Complete place and date on the signature page; the Effective Date is the date of the last signature.
  4. Have a licensed French attorney read the final text before either side signs — this document is a starting point, not advice.
  5. Exchange two signed originals (wet ink or a reliable electronic signature); each Party keeps one.
Five decisions, in order. Everything else in the draft is already settled and internally consistent.

And

Galerie Lumière SARL, a limited liability company (société à responsabilité limitée) organised under the laws of France, with a share capital of €40,000, registered with the Trade and Companies Register (RCS) of Paris under number 845 210 396, having its registered office at 27 rue de Turenne, 75003 Paris, France, represented by Mr Julien Mercier, in his capacity as Manager (Gérant), duly empowered for the purposes hereof (“Galerie Lumière”);

each a “Party” and together the “Parties”.

Recitals

(A)Atelier Nord designs and manufactures handcrafted lighting and homeware in Lille. Galerie Lumière operates retail gallery-boutiques in Paris.

(B)The Parties wish to explore a potential retail partnership under which Galerie Lumière would present and sell Atelier Nord collections in its retail spaces, possibly including a co-branded line (the negotiations and evaluation together, as further defined below, the “Purpose”).

(C)In the course of these discussions, each Party expects to disclose to the other information of a commercial, financial, technical or creative nature that it treats as confidential, and the Parties wish to record the terms on which such information will be protected.

Now, therefore, the Parties agree as follows:

Article 1 Definitions

1.1“Confidential Information” means any and all information, in whatever form or medium (oral, written, electronic, visual, or embodied in samples, prototypes or premises), disclosed by or on behalf of one Party (the “Disclosing Party”) to the other Party (the “Receiving Party”) in connection with the Purpose, whether before or after the Effective Date, that is identified as confidential at the time of disclosure or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential from its nature or the circumstances of its disclosure. Confidential Information includes, without limitation, business and marketing plans, pricing and margin structures, supplier and customer lists, sales figures, designs, sketches, prototypes, manufacturing know-how, trade secrets (secrets des affaires within the meaning of Article L.151-1 of the French Commercial Code), and the existence, status and content of the Parties’ discussions.

1.2“Purpose” means the evaluation, discussion and negotiation of a potential retail partnership for the presentation and sale of Atelier Nord collections through Galerie Lumière’s retail spaces, including a possible co-branded product line, and any preparatory work reasonably related thereto.

1.3“Representatives” means, in respect of a Party, its corporate officers, directors, employees, and professional advisers bound by a duty of confidentiality (including counsel and accountants), in each case only to the extent they need to know Confidential Information for the Purpose.

1.4“Effective Date” means the date on which the last of the two Parties signs this Agreement.

Note Broad by design — no marking requirement, so showroom conversations and unmarked prototypes are covered. The carve-outs in Article 3 do the balancing.

Article 2 Confidentiality obligations

2.1The Receiving Party shall keep the Disclosing Party’s Confidential Information strictly confidential and shall protect it with at least the same degree of care it applies to its own confidential information of a similar nature, and in no event less than a reasonable degree of care.

2.2The Receiving Party shall use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose whatsoever, including any purpose competitive with or detrimental to the Disclosing Party.

2.3The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information only to those of its Representatives who need to know it for the Purpose, provided such Representatives are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as this Agreement. The Receiving Party remains fully liable for any act or omission of its Representatives that would constitute a breach of this Agreement if committed by the Receiving Party itself.

2.4The Receiving Party shall not copy or reproduce Confidential Information beyond what is reasonably necessary for the Purpose. All copies shall reproduce any confidentiality legends appearing on the original.

2.5The Receiving Party shall notify the Disclosing Party promptly in writing upon becoming aware of any unauthorised use or disclosure of Confidential Information, and shall reasonably cooperate to limit its consequences.

Article 3 Exclusions

3.1Confidential Information does not include information that the Receiving Party can demonstrate:

(a)is or becomes generally available to the public other than through a breach of this Agreement by the Receiving Party or its Representatives;

(b)was already lawfully known to the Receiving Party, free of any confidentiality restriction, before disclosure by the Disclosing Party;

(c)was independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party’s Confidential Information; or

(d)was lawfully received from a third party that, to the Receiving Party’s knowledge, owed no duty of confidentiality to the Disclosing Party in respect of it.

3.2The Receiving Party bears the burden of proving that an exclusion applies, by contemporaneous written records.

Note The four classic exclusions, plus the burden-of-proof line that most short NDAs forget — without 3.2, every dispute starts with an argument about who must prove what.

Article 4 Compelled disclosure

4.1The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to the extent required by applicable law, regulation, or a binding order of a court or competent authority, provided that it: (i) gives the Disclosing Party prompt prior written notice, to the extent legally permitted, so that the Disclosing Party may seek a protective measure; (ii) reasonably cooperates with any such effort at the Disclosing Party’s expense; and (iii) discloses only that portion of the Confidential Information it is legally required to disclose.

Article 5 No licence — no commitment

5.1All Confidential Information remains the property of the Disclosing Party. Nothing in this Agreement grants the Receiving Party any right, title, licence or interest in or to any Confidential Information or any intellectual-property right of the Disclosing Party, by implication or otherwise.

5.2Confidential Information is provided “as is”. The Disclosing Party makes no representation or warranty as to its accuracy or completeness, and accepts no liability for the Receiving Party’s use of it, without prejudice to Article 8.

5.3Nothing in this Agreement obliges either Party to disclose any particular information, to continue discussions, or to enter into the contemplated partnership or any other agreement. Each Party bears its own costs of the discussions.

Note 5.3 keeps pre-contractual freedom explicit: either side may walk away from the talks without owing the other an explanation — only the confidentiality survives.

Article 6 Return or destruction

6.1Upon the Disclosing Party’s written request, or upon the end of the Parties’ discussions if earlier, the Receiving Party shall, at the Disclosing Party’s option, return or destroy all Confidential Information in its possession or control, including all copies, extracts and summaries, and shall confirm in writing that it has done so within fifteen (15) business days.

6.2The Receiving Party may retain one archival copy where and for as long as required by applicable law or bona fide internal compliance policy, and copies held in automatic electronic back-ups, provided all retained material remains subject to this Agreement for as long as it is retained.

Article 7 Term and survival

7.1This Agreement takes effect on the Effective Date and remains in force for a term of three (3) years. The obligations of confidentiality and restricted use in Articles 2 to 6 apply throughout that term to all Confidential Information disclosed during it.

7.2Notwithstanding Article 7.1, information that constitutes a trade secret (secret des affaires) within the meaning of Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Commercial Code remains protected under this Agreement for as long as it continues to qualify as such.

7.3Expiry of this Agreement does not affect accrued rights or liabilities, nor the provisions of Articles 8, 10 and 11, which survive.

Note Three years flat, per the brief — longer than the French 24-month habit, and trade secrets outlive the term via 7.2. See drafting note 3.

Article 8 Remedies

8.1Each Party acknowledges that a breach of this Agreement may cause the Disclosing Party harm that damages alone cannot adequately repair.

8.2Accordingly, in the event of a breach or threatened breach, the Disclosing Party may seek any interim or protective measure available under French law, including summary proceedings before the judge for interim relief (juge des référés), without prejudice to any claim for damages or any other remedy available at law.

8.3No failure or delay by a Party in exercising any right under this Agreement operates as a waiver of that right.

Note The référé is the remedy that matters in practice — it stops the leak in days. Damages come later, if ever. No penalty clause here; see drafting note 4.

Article 9 Non-solicitation [option]

9.1[During the term of this Agreement and for twelve (12) months thereafter, neither Party shall directly solicit for employment or engagement any employee of the other Party with whom it had material contact in connection with the Purpose, without the other Party’s prior written consent.

9.2This Article does not prohibit general recruitment advertising not targeted at the other Party’s personnel, nor the hiring of a person who responds to such advertising or approaches a Party on their own initiative.]

Option Clause de non-sollicitation — deliberately bracketed, not default. Keep it if the teams will mix on the shop floor; strike it if it would chill normal hiring. Either way, both signatories initial the choice in this margin. French courts read these clauses strictly — the narrow scope (12 months, material contact only) is what keeps it enforceable.

Article 10 General provisions

10.1Entire agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties concerning the confidentiality of the discussions and supersedes any prior understanding on that subject. It may be amended only in writing signed by both Parties.

10.2No assignment. Neither Party may assign or transfer this Agreement, in whole or in part, without the other Party’s prior written consent.

10.3Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid or unenforceable, the remainder continues in force, and the Parties shall replace the affected provision with a valid one reflecting their original intent as closely as possible.

10.4No partnership. Nothing in this Agreement creates any partnership, agency, joint venture or exclusive relationship between the Parties.

10.5Notices. Notices under this Agreement shall be in writing and delivered to the registered office of the addressee by hand against receipt, by registered letter with acknowledgment of receipt (lettre recommandée avec accusé de réception), or by e-mail confirmed by one of the foregoing.

10.6Counterparts; signature. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, together constituting one instrument, and may be signed electronically; a reliable electronic signature within the meaning of Articles 1366 and 1367 of the French Civil Code has the same evidential force as a handwritten one.

10.7Language. The Parties confirm their express wish that this Agreement be drafted in the English language, each having had the opportunity to seek a French translation.

10.8Personal data. Each Party processes the business contact details of the other Party’s personnel as an independent controller, solely for managing the relationship contemplated by this Agreement, in accordance with applicable data-protection law.

Article 11 Governing law — jurisdiction

11.1This Agreement is governed by and shall be construed in accordance with French law.

11.2Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, including its validity, interpretation, performance or termination, that the Parties cannot resolve amicably within thirty (30) days of written notice shall be submitted to the exclusive jurisdiction of the commercial court (tribunal de commerce) of Paris, notwithstanding plurality of defendants or third-party proceedings, and without prejudice to Article 8.2.

Note Paris, not Lille: the amicable-resolution window is short on purpose, and the carve-back to 8.2 keeps the référé available immediately.

Drafting notes

1.Why mutual. Retail talks are two-way: Atelier Nord opens its costings and unreleased designs; Galerie Lumière opens its footfall, margins and customer knowledge. Symmetric obligations (Article 2) protect both flows with one paper, instead of a second NDA three weeks in.

2.No marking requirement. Clause 1.1 protects what a reasonable person would understand to be confidential, not only stamped documents — showroom conversations and prototypes rarely get stamped. The balance comes from the four carve-outs of Article 3 and the written-records burden in 3.2.

3.The three-year term. Longer than the 24 months customary in short French NDAs, shorter than the five years often asked in US paper — sized to roughly two collection cycles. And clause 7.2 stops the term from silently expiring real trade secrets: those stay protected as long as they qualify under Article L.151-1 of the Commercial Code.

4.Remedies without a penalty clause. Article 8 relies on interim relief (référé) plus actual damages. A clause pénale (Article 1231-5 of the Civil Code) is available in France but invites judicial revision and hard bargaining at the wrong moment; add one only if a counterparty has a history of leaks.

5.The bracketed non-solicit. Article 9 is an option, not a default, because the two teams will mingle at fairs and on the shop floor, and French courts read non-solicitation strictly. The scope is kept proportionate — twelve months, personnel with material contact only, general advertising carved out — so the clause holds up if the Parties keep it.

6.English text, French law. Valid between two French commercial parties, and convenient if a non-French investor or partner later joins the talks. Clause 10.7 records the deliberate choice of language, and the French legal terms of art are anchored in italics where the English gloss is imperfect.
