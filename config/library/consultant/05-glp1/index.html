02 / The Mechanism

One hormone, four effects, and why it works

1

Mimics a gut hormone (GLP-1)

These drugs copy glucagon-like peptide-1, released by the gut after eating. Next-gen molecules also hit the GIP and glucagon receptors — the “dual” and “triple” agonists.

2

Signals satiety to the brain

It tells the brain you are full and slows how fast the stomach empties. Appetite drops; portions shrink; snacking falls. This is the lever that reshapes food demand downstream.

3

Improves blood-sugar control

It boosts insulin when glucose is high and blunts glucagon — the diabetes heritage of the class (Ozempic, Mounjaro) before obesity became the bigger prize.

4

The catch: keep taking it

Effect persists only while dosed. Stop, and appetite and weight tend to return — the commercial engine (recurring revenue) and the clinical weakness (adherence) are the same fact.

The deal

Most-Favored-Nation pricing

A Nov-2025 agreement between the Trump administration, Lilly and Novo tied U.S. GLP-1 prices toward the lowest developed-market level, launching via the TrumpRx portal — the driver behind the cuts above.

The fight

The compounding crackdown

With shortages over, the FDA moved (Apr 2026) to strip semaglutide & tirzepatide from the 503B bulk-compounding list — ending large-scale copies that had undercut brands. Now in Fifth Circuit litigation; 503A pharmacies may persist.

Sources: TrumpRx / HealthFacts Journal (2026); CNBC (Dec 2025); NPR (May 6, 2026); AMCP; Orrick & Pharmacy Times (May 2026); Courthouse News. Prices are intro/dose-dependent.

Why it decides the race

Making it is harder than approving it

  • 1Injectable GLP-1s need large, specialised capex (fill/finish, device assembly) — a multi-year, multi-billion barrier that even generics face.
  • 2The 2022–24 shortage is the cautionary tale: unmet demand fed compounders and eroded pricing power. Capacity is now the defence.
  • 3Oral pills (orforglipron is small-molecule, not a peptide) ease this — cheaper to scale, no cold chain — reshaping who can supply the next 20M patients.

10 / What Can Break It

Four risks that bound the thesis

01

Adherence & durability

Over half discontinue within year one; ~three-quarters by year two. Weight tends to return off-drug. GI side effects hit a large share; muscle-loss concerns are live. Real-world value hinges on staying on it.

02

The patent cliff

Semaglutide's core U.S. patent expires 2032 — but China's lapses in 2026, with India, Brazil, Canada following. Generic “Nozempic” supply in ex-U.S. markets is already reshaping Novo's 2026 guidance.

03

Political pricing pressure

The MFN deal cut U.S. list-adjacent prices hard. Further government leverage — Medicare, MFN extensions — could compress margins faster than volume growth offsets, especially for the injectable base.

04

Crowding & commoditisation

Amgen, Roche, Viking and Chinese entrants (mazdutide, ribupatide) are massing behind the leaders. As efficacy converges near ~20%, differentiation narrows to price, route and side-effects — a margin risk for everyone.

Sources: AJMC; ScienceDirect; PMC adherence studies (2025–26); Grand View Research & C&EN (patent cliff); Novo Nordisk Q1-2026 guidance; company pipelines.

11 / The Outlook

Where this goes — a reasoned view

The gold rush is real, not a bubble — but the prize concentrates. Expect a durable duopoly, an oral-pill land-grab through 2027, and a widening gap between whoever can make it and defend the price.

Call 01 · The leaders

Lilly's lead widens; Novo's fight is the pill

Tirzepatide's efficacy + Foundayo's oral reach compound Lilly's momentum. Novo's counter is the fastest oral-Wegovy ramp on record and CagriSema — a 2026 verdict.

Call 02 · The challengers

Watch the pill & the amylins

Viking as an M&A / manufacturing prize; Amgen's monthly MariTide on convenience; Roche's amylin on tolerability; China as a volume & price wildcard.

Call 03 · The adjacencies

Price the shockwave, not just the drug

F&B mix-shift and device displacement are slow-burn re-ratings — position on the derivatives (protein, satiety, sleep) as much as the two pharma names.
