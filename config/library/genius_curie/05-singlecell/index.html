About this Single-cell atlas — what it is, and how to read it

Why

To let you see the diseased tissue the way a single-cell lab does — every cell a point, coloured by what kind of cell it is — for a rare neurodegenerative disease, ALS.

Context

A faithful reconstruction of the human primary motor cortex atlas in Pineda et al., Cell 2024 (~380k nuclei, 64 donors). Reported clusters, proportions and canonical markers — not the raw download.

How to use it

Click a cluster (plot or legend) for its markers + function. Toggle colour by gene to recolour every cell by one gene's expression. Switch Control ↔ ALS to see who is lost.

What I assumed

NeuN-sorted (neuron-enriched) proportions per the study; a representative embedding generated to respect the reported cluster structure. Coordinates are illustrative; identities, markers and the biology are real.

The study
Pineda SS, Lee H, Ulloa-Navas MJ, et al. Single-cell dissection of the human motor and prefrontal cortices in ALS and FTLD. Cell 2024;187(8):1971–1989.e16.
~380,000 nuclei 64 donors 46 subtypes DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.02.031 GEO GSE174332 PMID 38521060

Cell-class taxonomy & markers follow the BICCN human M1 reference — Bakken TE et al., Nature 2021;598:111–119 (PMC8494640). Betz cells sit in the L5 ET class; in human M1, GABAergic neurons are ~33% of neurons.

Research and education — not medical advice. This is a representative reconstruction for teaching how a single-cell atlas is read; point coordinates are generated to respect the reported cluster structure, not downloaded. For analysis, use the primary data (GEO GSE174332). ALS is a rare disease (prevalence ≈ 5–9 / 100,000).
