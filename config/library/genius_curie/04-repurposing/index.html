Saracatinib for FOP — a docked candidate, argued

The medicinal-chemistry case for repurposing saracatinib (AZD0530), an AstraZeneca Src/Abl oncology inhibitor, as an ALK2 / ACVR1 kinase inhibitor for fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva — the molecule, its measured properties, why the oncology hinge-binder lands on the FOP receptor, and the real trial that put it in patients.

Why
To make the repurposing argument for saracatinib in FOP legible as a med-chem case: a real molecule with real, sourced descriptors, a mechanistic reason it hits ALK2, and the clinical trail that followed — the kind of one-page brief that decides whether a repurposing lead is worth a wet-lab campaign.
Context
FOP is driven by the ACVR1 R206H neofunction (hyperactive BMP–SMAD1/5/8 signalling through the ALK2 kinase). Saracatinib was built for oncology (Src/Abl), shelved after weak Phase II cancer signals, and later found to be a potent ATP-competitive ALK2 inhibitor — a molecule that has already cleared human safety in 600+ subjects.

02

Why an oncology Src inhibitor lands on ALK2

the repurposing rationale — shared ATP pocket, hinge chemistry, and measured selectivity

Saracatinib is a 4-anilinoquinazoline — the same privileged scaffold that made gefitinib/erlotinib potent, tuned by AstraZeneca to hit the ATP sites of the tyrosine kinases Src (c-Src IC50 ≈ 2.7 nM) and Abl (≈ 30 nM). The scaffold's whole job is to sit in a kinase ATP cleft and hydrogen-bond to the hinge. ALK2 is a different family (a type-I BMP receptor serine/threonine kinase), but it has the same ATP fold and the same class of hinge — so a molecule optimised to occupy one ATP pocket can, by chemical accident, occupy the other. That accident is what a phenotypic/bioactive-compound screen for ALK2 inhibition surfaced.

2·1 ATP-competitive, hinge-anchored

All reported ALK2 inhibitors bind the ATP pocket and donate a hydrogen bond to the hinge amide of His286 (Bullock lab, structural series). Saracatinib's quinazoline N1 is the canonical hinge acceptor of the anilinoquinazoline class; the 4-anilino N–H is its single donor. It is a textbook type-I binder — it recognises the active-like conformation, competing directly with cellular ATP.

2·2 Potency that survived the jump

Against the FOP neofunction, saracatinib inhibits ALK2 with a cellular IC50 ≈ 15 nM (in-cell immunofluorescence, Williams et al., JCI Insight 2018). That is not a weak off-target whisper — it is low-nanomolar, in the same regime as its oncology on-targets. Crucially it is selective over the other type-I receptors: ALK3/4/5/6 are spared, so it blocks the disease kinase without collapsing every BMP/TGF-β signal.

03

Where it sits — the ACVR1 ATP pocket

binding rationale into the ALK2 kinase domain (UniProt Q04771 · kinase domain 208–502)

The argument for the pose is structural, not hand-waved: the crystallography of the ALK2 series (e.g. RCSB PDB 5OXG, ALK2 + LDN-212854) shows type-I inhibitors threading the cleft between the N- and C-lobes, pinned by a single hinge hydrogen bond to His286 and shaped by the gatekeeper Thr283. Saracatinib's quinazoline reproduces exactly that contact set. Below: the pocket, schematically, with the residues that matter.

His286 — hinge
Backbone amide donates/accepts the single defining hinge H-bond to the quinazoline N1. Shared anchor of every published ALK2 inhibitor.
Thr283 — gatekeeper
Small residue gating the back pocket; its size permits the chloro-benzodioxole to reach the hydrophobic pocket behind the hinge.
Lys235 — catalytic
β3 lysine of the ATP machinery; water-mediated contacts here help order selective inhibitors in ALK2's inactive-favoured cleft.
Asp336 — DFG
Start of the activation loop / Mg²⁺-coordinating aspartate; a type-I binder occupies the ATP site with DFG-in.
GS domain 178–207
The regulatory glycine-serine domain. R206H sits at its C-terminus — the mutation that unleashes the neofunction the drug must silence.
Kinase domain 208–502
The catalytic module targeted here. Structure basis: ALK2 crystallography incl. PDB 5OXG; the ATP cleft is the druggable surface.

Residue numbering and domain boundaries: UniProt Q04771. Hinge/gatekeeper interaction pattern generalised from the ALK2 inhibitor crystallographic series (refs 4–5). This is a binding rationale anchored to homologous structures, not a saracatinib–ALK2 co-crystal — see limits.

04

Oncology → FOP — the second life of a shelved drug

the origin-vs-new-use story · a twice-repurposed molecule

The repurposing value proposition is blunt: saracatinib has already been given to 600+ humans and has a known safety and pharmacokinetic profile. It failed to earn an oncology approval — and that failure is exactly what makes it cheap, fast and low-risk to redeploy against a mechanism it happens to hit hard.

2000s · ORIGIN
Built by AstraZeneca as a Src/Abl inhibitor for cancer
Designed as an orally available dual c-Src (2.7 nM) / Abl (30 nM) inhibitor for solid tumours. Phase I set tolerated doses (≈175 mg; 125 mg in Japanese patients).
EARLY 2010s · THE MISS
Weak Phase II oncology signal — shelved
Across several tumour types the efficacy signal was low. The molecule was clinically de-risked but commercially stranded — an ideal repurposing substrate: safe in humans, no home indication.
MID 2010s · FIRST REPURPOSE
Alzheimer's disease — Fyn kinase
Repurposed toward Fyn (a Src-family kinase) to counter amyloid-β/tau synaptic toxicity; trialled in AD. Establishes the pattern — a Src-family binder pointed at a new disease.
2018 → NOW · SECOND REPURPOSE (this dossier)
FOP — potent, selective ALK2 inhibition
A bioactive-compound screen flagged saracatinib as a low-nanomolar ALK2 inhibitor (IC50 15 nM), efficacious in the ACVR1 Q207D heterotopic-ossification mouse model, and selective over ALK3/4/5/6. It moved into the human STOPFOP trial — a genuine "dual drug repurposing" story (oncology → AD → FOP).

5·1 Preclinical proof

Williams, Bocobo, Bullock et al. (JCI Insight, 2018) established the case: saracatinib inhibits mutant ALK2 at 15 nM in cells, is selective over the other type-I receptors, and prevents heterotopic bone in the ACVR1 Q207D transgenic mouse — the in-vivo bridge from biochemistry to a disease phenotype.

5·2 The registered protocol

The STOPFOP protocol (Smilde, Botman, Eekhoff et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders, 2022) fixed the design above — CT-measured heterotopic bone as the objective primary endpoint, the R206H-only population, and the natural-history-controlled extension. Registered as NCT04307953.

Honesty — the gap between a lead and a treatment

  • This is a docked rationale, not a co-crystal. The pose argument rests on the ALK2 inhibitor crystallographic series (PDB 5OXG and relatives) and the shared 4-anilinoquinazoline hinge chemistry — not on a published saracatinib·ALK2 structure. A co-crystal or cryo-EM would be the confirming experiment.
  • Descriptors are computed, potencies are assay-specific. The 15 nM ALK2 IC50 is a cellular immunofluorescence readout; the Src/Abl values are enzymatic. They are not interchangeable, and the ALK2 selectivity window (4.8× over activin) is real but modest — off-target activin/TGF-β inhibition is not zero.
  • The clinical result is not in. STOPFOP was designed and run, but at the last public update no results were posted and status was listed as unknown. This dossier makes the chemistry case; it does not claim the trial succeeded. Saracatinib is investigational for FOP — not an approved therapy.
  • What would confirm it, in order: (1) a saracatinib–ALK2 co-structure; (2) a clean kinome-wide selectivity panel at therapeutic exposure; (3) the STOPFOP CT endpoint. Only the third tells you it helps patients.
