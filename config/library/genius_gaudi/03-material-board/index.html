Why

You have a warm Paris apartment and a feeling about it. This is the shoppable half of the design package — not "warm woods and soft textiles" in the abstract, but the actual materials and the actual pieces, priced, so you can buy the room.

Context

A ~72 m² Haussmann flat, north light, existing oak floors kept. Register is organic warm-modern — curve over rule, light as a material, beauty pulled from honest stone and salvaged tile. The budget is a real retail estimate at Paris prices (incl. VAT), not a quote.

How to use it

Read the six material swatches first — they set the language. Then the shopping list: filter by room to plan one space at a time, and flip the Value ⇄ Premium switch to re-total the whole board against a spend. The ledger on the right always sums the visible pieces.

What I assumed

A mid-to-upper retail tier, floors and walls already sound (surfaces & finishes only — no structure, no joinery labour), two people living here, and that you'd rather own a few honest things than many thin ones. Change any of it and I re-cost in place.

02 The colour story Warm neutrals hold the light; one earth carries the eye.

The room is built of plaster and honey — an ecru so warm it reads as light itself, oak the colour of afternoon, travertine's pale sand. Against that quiet, a single burnt terracotta does all the speaking: a sofa, a cushion, a glazed edge of tile. Brass threads through it like a drawn line — thin, warm, never gold-loud.

This is the discipline: let the neutrals be the walls of the composition and spend all your colour on one earth. The terracotta is the fire; everything else is the hearth around it. Nothing here fights the north light — it gathers it, holds it, and gives it back the colour of clay.
