Why

You asked to redesign a Haussmann living room without touching its bones — the mouldings, the point-de-Hongrie floor, the tall window. This sheet is the design intent your architect or joiner builds from.

Context

24 m², one long façade wall with a single tall casement, a chimney breast on the opposite wall, two doors. Today the room fights itself — a sofa marooned mid-floor, light spilling onto no one.

How to use it

Read the BEFORE / AFTER plans to scale, hover the elements list to place each piece, study the elevation, then hand the palette and the three moves to whoever fits it out.

What I assumed

Mid-to-upper budget, a warm organic register (curves, oak, limewash, brass), renter-safe where possible. No wall moved. Costs are indicative €, not a quote. Structure is an engineer's call, not mine.

05

Zoning — why the room bends

One room, three uses, no walls. The zones are drawn by furniture and light, and they overlap the way rooms in a life do.

The read, in the architect’s voice

The room came to me straight — a good ceiling, an honest floor, and a tall window pouring its light onto no one. A sofa had been left marooned in the middle like a boat with no shore, and the traffic cut clean through where people were meant to rest. The straight line was doing what the straight line always does when nobody watches it: wasting the corners and fighting the light.

So the room bends toward the window. Not for decoration — because that is where the light lives, and light is the one material this apartment already owns and pays nothing for. I gave it a light-well: a curved sofa, the shape a chain makes hanging under its own weight, opening its arms to the glass so the sun lands on shoulders and open books instead of bare parquet. The dead corner beside it, where nothing had ever sat, becomes the best seat in the room — a reading chair standing exactly in the daylight.

Against it, low and warm, the hearth: the chimney breast everyone had been trained to ignore, given a spine of curved built-ins that round its hard corners and pull the eye down to the fire. And where a right angle had been left to gather dust, a round table — for dining, for working, for the friends who arrive — because a circle seats more and turns strangers to face each other. The circulation, once a blade through the middle, now runs one clean loop around a centre. You will walk the room without ever crossing where someone sits.

Three zones, no walls between them, breathing into one another as the day turns. The mouldings stayed, the floor stayed, the window stayed. Everything they held simply learned to curve toward the light.

— Gaudí, architect SPLIT · design intent, not stamped construction documents

06

Three moves that transform the room

Everything above resolves to three decisions. Do only these and the room already breathes differently.

Move 01

Curve the seating toward the light

Lift the sofa off the middle of the floor and give it the shape of a chain hanging free — a soft arc facing the tall window. The light stops falling on nothing; it falls on the people who came to sit in it.

Gain · +2 usable seats, north-west light captured
Move 02

Make the chimney breast the spine

Stop ignoring the fireplace. Wrap it in curved joinery that rounds its hard corners and climbs to the corniche — storage, display, and a low warm anchor that pulls the whole room down toward the fire and gives the sofa something to face away from.

Gain · 3.6 lin-m of storage, a true focal wall
Move 03

Round the dead corner into a table

Where a right angle sat empty gathering dust, drop a round pedestal table. A circle seats more than a square, has no corner to bruise a hip, and turns the whole party to face one another. Dining, work, homework, wine — one honest circle does all four.

Gain · a 4-seat table from wasted floor
