Why

You asked for a vivid long weekend in Marrakech from Paris, for two: the medina and souks, the Jardin Majorelle, a riad stay, a High Atlas / Ourika day, and an Agafay desert-edge sunset. This is that trip, day by day, bookable — every place, price and link is real and current.

Context

Four days, Thursday to Sunday, two travellers out of Paris-CDG. The medina is walked (cars don't go where you're going); the two day-trips (Ourika, Agafay) are door-to-door transfers from your riad. Budget is built for two and runs in three tiers you can flip live.

How to use it

Read the days in order — each is clustered so you never cross the city twice. Tap any map pin or booking button (they open the real page). Flip the Shoestring / Comfort / Splurge tier in the budget to re-price the whole trip. Confirm hours & prices at booking; a medina in July keeps its own clock.

What I assumed

July shoulder heat (plan mornings, rest the afternoon, live on rooftops after dark), a riad in the medina over a Gueliz hotel, one Atlas day and one dune sundown rather than a Sahara overnight, and card-and-cash both (much of the souk is cash, small dirham notes).

Four days, hour by hour

Clustered so you never cross the red city twice. Mornings for the sights before the heat and the crowds; afternoons slow; the rooftops after dark. What to skip is named as plainly as what to see.

1THU
arrive · the red walls close in

Into the medina & the souks

Base: your riad, inside the walls · all on foot
13:30

Land at Menara (RAK) ≈3h30 from CDG

Have the riad send a driver — the medina is a maze of car-free derbs and you won't find the door with a suitcase. A private airport transfer runs about 150–200 MAD (€14–18); agree it when you book the room.

15 min to the medina gate, then porter on foot
15:00

Drop bags, mint tea on the roof

Settle in, let the riad pour the first tea, and don't fight the afternoon heat — July in Marrakech is for shade until five. Learn your door: photograph the derb and the nearest landmark so you can find your way back after dark.

17:00

First walk into the souks cash · small notes

Ease in from Souk Semmarine up toward the dyers' and the coppersmiths' lanes — the souks are a covered warren, so let yourself get a little lost while it's still light. Don't buy on day one; learn the prices. Haggling is expected — offer a third, settle near half.

19:30

Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk the great square

Jemaa el-Fnaa only truly wakes at nightfall — smoke off the grill stalls, snake-charmers packing up, orange-juice carts (No. 1 is the local shorthand). Eat a bowl of harira and a skewer standing up at a numbered stall; it's theatre as much as dinner.

skip → the carts pushing photos with monkeys and chained snakes — it's a shakedown and the animals are mistreated. Walk on; the grills are the point.
21:00

Rooftop nightcap over the square

Climb to a terrace for the view down onto the smoke and lanterns — a café on the square's edge for a mint tea, or push into the souk to Nomad's roof if you want a proper table. Then follow your photographed landmarks home.

tonight's one thing → stand in the middle of Jemaa el-Fnaa at full dark and just turn once, slowly. Eight hundred years of this square doing exactly this. That's the trip, right there — everything else is detail.
2FRI
the blue garden & the palaces

Majorelle, Bahia & Ben Youssef

Gueliz in the morning, medina palaces after · petit-taxi + foot
08:15

Jardin Majorelle at opening 08:00–18:30 · 170 MAD

Be at the gate when Jardin Majorelle opens — by ten the queues wrap the block and the famous Majorelle-blue villa is elbow-to-elbow. Buy the timed ticket in advance on the official site only (170 MAD garden; the copycats overcharge). A petit-taxi from the medina is ~30–40 MAD; insist on the meter or agree the price first.

~12 min by petit-taxi to Gueliz
09:45

Musée YSL, next door 10:00–18:30 · closed Wed · 140 MAD

The Musée Yves Saint Laurent shares the wall — one terracotta-brick building, a single exquisite room of couture, well air-conditioned. A good hour before the day heats up. (Same official ticket site sells a combined pass.)

note → the Berber Museum inside the garden is closed 6–12 July 2026 for its annual rotation, reopening the 13th — check your dates on the official site.
12:30

Lunch & the heat of the day

Long garden lunch in Gueliz, then back to the riad for the worst of the afternoon — a swim in the plunge pool, a nap under the fan. This is not laziness; it's how the city is designed to be lived in July.

16:30

Bahia Palace 08:00–17:00 · 70 MAD

Back into the medina for the Bahia Palace — painted cedar ceilings, zellige courtyards, the harem quarters cool and quiet late in the day. Go near closing when the tour groups have gone; the light through the riad courtyards is best then. (Last entry ~16:15 — don't cut it fine.)

17:45

Ben Youssef Madrasa 09:00–19:00 · 50 MAD

A ten-minute walk north to the Ben Youssef Madrasa, the 16th-century Qur'anic school — the most breathtaking tilework in the city, a still courtyard pool, carved cedar and stucco to the ceiling. Restored and open late; catch it in the last of the daylight.

10-min walk between the two, through the souk's northern edge
20:00

Dinner on a medina rooftop

End on a terrace over the ochre roofs — La Terrasse des Épices (they serve wine, rare in the medina) or the garden-riad calm of Le Jardin. Order the pastilla once while you're here — the sweet-savoury pigeon pie is the dish people fly back for.

walking logic → today front-loads Gueliz (Majorelle + YSL are neighbours) in the cool morning, then keeps the two medina palaces (Bahia + Ben Youssef) together in the late afternoon so you cross town only once, in a taxi, not twice on foot.
3SAT
up into the mountains

Ourika Valley & the High Atlas

Full-day trip · ~65 km each way · private driver from the riad
08:30

Leave the city, climb the valley ~90 min drive

A private driver collects you at the riad and heads south into the Ourika Valley — terraced fields, red-earth villages, the Atlas rising green and wet against the desert. Book a private day (~€70–90 for the car, or a shared seat from ~€25pp); see the day-trips card below.

~65 km up the R203 into the mountains
10:00

An argan co-op & mint tea

Stop at a women's argan cooperative on the way up — watch the nuts cracked and pressed by hand, taste amlou (the argan-almond-honey butter), buy the oil at source if you want it. Twenty minutes, genuinely worth it, and the money stays in the valley.

11:00

Setti Fatma & the waterfalls 1,500 m · Berber village

Setti Fatma sits at the head of the valley — hire a local guide at the village (100–200 MAD) for the scramble to the first of the seven cascades, about 45 minutes up over rock and little bridges. Wear real shoes; it's a proper clamber, not a stroll.

skip → the "guides" who latch on before you've agreed a price or the route. Fix the guide, the price and which waterfalls at the village start, in writing on your phone if you must.
13:30

Tagine over the river

Lunch at one of the riverside terraces with tables set literally in the shallows — a slow-cooked tagine and a cold drink with your feet near the water (60–100 MAD a plate). The valley air is ten degrees cooler than the city; this is the reprieve you came up for.

15:30

The long descent, a viewpoint or two

Wander back down with a couple of photo stops — a Berber village, a viewpoint over the terraces. If your dates land on a Monday there's a genuine country souk near the valley mouth; on a Saturday, just enjoy the quiet road down.

17:30

Back to the riad, an easy night

Home by early evening, dusty and content. Tonight is a quiet one — a light dinner near the riad, an early night. Tomorrow ends in the desert and you'll want the legs.

carnet note → Ourika is the popular Atlas day (closest, greenest, the waterfalls). If you'd rather trade waterfalls for real summits and a Berber village walk under Toubkal, ask me to swap this day for Imlil — same driver, wilder valley, harder walk. Either is a real day; this one's the softer, wetter one.
4SUN
last souk, then the stones at sundown

Souks by day, Agafay at dusk

Morning in the medina · sunset transfer to the desert · late flight
09:00

The souk, this time to buy cash · haggle

Now you know the prices. Go back for the one or two things worth carrying home — a leather pouf (they'll ship it), a Berber rug from a co-op, a bag of ras-el-hanout from a spice stall, a hand-hammered brass lamp. Start at a third of the ask, walk if it stalls; the second price is always better.

11:30

A last mint tea, check out

One more tea on the riad roof, pack the pouf, settle the bill. Leave the bags with the riad — most will mind them for the day so you're not dragging a suitcase into the desert.

13:00

Lunch, then rest before the dunes

A slow lunch and the last of the afternoon shade. The desert trip is an evening thing; there's no rush, and you'll be out late.

17:00

Transfer to the Agafay desert ~40 min · transfer included

A pickup runs you 40 minutes out to the Agafay — not sand dunes but a lunar sweep of grey rock desert on the city's edge, the Atlas snow-capped on the horizon. Book a sunset camel + dinner combo (from ~€35pp, transfer included; see the card below).

~30 km southwest, transfer included in the package
18:30

Camel at golden hour, dinner under the stars

A short camel walk along a Berber path as the sun drops, then dinner at a desert camp — tagine, a fire, drums, and a sky with no city in it. This is the send-off: the last light going pink on the Atlas, mint tea, the stones going cold.

22:00

Collect bags, fly home RAK → CDG

Back to the riad for the luggage and out to Menara. If your flight's an early Monday instead, the camp will still have you back by 22:00 for a good night's sleep — either way, agree the airport run when you book the room.

the send-off → Agafay isn't the Sahara — that's a two-day drive and a different trip. But for a long weekend it's the honest trade: real desert silence, a real camel, the Atlas going gold, and you're back for your flight. Sand-dune purists, ask me for the Merzouga overnight version and we'll stretch the trip.
